### Build an AI assistant that actually knows you — step by step, no coding

**By Alex Wakeland** — journeyman lineman, not a coder. Written with Castle, the AI assistant this book teaches you to build.

Most people use AI like a vending machine: type a question, get an answer, forgotten by morning. This book builds the other thing — an assistant that **remembers your life, follows your written rules, runs standing jobs on a schedule, and shows its work so you can check it.** No coding. No expensive stack. The difference between a chatbot and an assistant is not a better model; it is a handful of plain files and the habits that keep them true.

## The method, in one page

1. **Name it and write its character.** A one-page "soul file" that says how it talks, what it never does, and what it protects.
2. **Four files make it yours.** Soul (how it acts) · Identity (who it is) · User (who you are) · Memory (what it must never forget). Starter templates are in [`templates/`](templates/) — free to use.
3. **Keep the record true.** Facts get written down the day they happen. Summaries drift, so important facts get checked against their source before they're repeated.
4. **Rules of the house.** It never sends, posts, spends, or promises in your name without your word. It drafts; you dispose. The bigger the consequence, the clearer the yes.
5. **Standing jobs.** Every job gets an owner, a schedule or trigger, a done-condition, and a report shape — or it's a wish, not a job.
6. **Trust, but verify.** A report is testimony, not evidence. Check the decisive property at the source, and verify before the irreversible step, not after it.
7. **Your data is yours.** Keep your core files in two homes, export on a schedule, and be able to rebuild your assistant from your own copies.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know how to code?**
No. The author is a lineman. Every step is writing plain documents and setting up plain habits in a normal AI app.

**Which AI should I build on?**
Any major assistant you will actually open every day. The book's method — files, rules, jobs, verification — is platform-independent; the files are yours and move with you.

**Is this about prompts?**
No. One-off clever prompts are decoration. This book builds standing structure: a character file, a memory kept true, house rules, and jobs with done-conditions.

**How long does the build take?**
The first-week checklist runs about an hour a day for seven days, then two weeks of running and grading before you add anything. Reliability first, growth after.

**What does the paid book have that this repo doesn't?**
The full step-by-step for all nine chapters, the filled-in examples, the first-week checklist, the verification drills, and the chapter on growing into a second chair and your own local hardware.

**Who is Alex Wakeland?**
An IBEW journeyman lineman from the Quad Cities who runs his operation — business, content, and a household — with the assistant this book describes, and wrote the book with it.
